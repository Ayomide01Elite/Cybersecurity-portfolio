ShieldOps — DevSecOps Pipeline & SOAR Automation Platform

Expadox Labs · Team Project
Problem
Security scans typically run late — after deployment or during occasional pen tests — letting issues like hardcoded secrets and vulnerable dependencies reach production undetected.
Objective
Embed security scanning directly into CI/CD and automate the response to what it finds, instead of relying on manual review.
Architecture & Tools
SAST, DAST, dependency scanning, and secrets detection run on every commit; a SOAR layer enriches each finding with CVE/CVSS data, prioritizes it, and routes it to one of three automated playbooks (exposed secret, critical CVE, confirmed OWASP Top 10 flaw). Validated against a real, intentionally vulnerable test application.
My Contribution
I wrote the automated response playbook for the exposed-secrets finding type — the logic that determines how that specific kind of finding is triaged and routed once the pipeline catches it.
Results & Limitations
The team's dashboard tracks a mean-time-to-triage (MTTT) metric, reported by the team as faster than manual review.
Self-reported metric — not independently verified on this page; confirm with measured before/after data if available.
Lessons Learned
Writing response logic for a specific finding type (rather than just detecting it) requires thinking through false positives and escalation paths, not just the detection rule itself.

Endpoint Protection & Vulnerability Management Program

School-wide Phishing Attack Analysis & Student Guidance Report

Capstone Project · She Code Africa
Problem
Students are a high-risk, understudied demographic for phishing in the Nigerian context, despite heavy reliance on email and digital platforms.
Objective
Run a controlled phishing simulation to measure susceptibility, identify the technical and psychological indicators at play, and produce practical awareness guidance.
Architecture & Tools
A fake "EduSupport Foundation" scholarship email and landing page, built to exploit authority, scarcity, and social proof; sent to 25 participants via a controlled Slack channel. Simulated form data only — no real personal or financial information was collected or retained.
My Contribution
My task on the team was awareness creation — translating the simulation's findings into practical, plain-language phishing-awareness guidance for students.
Results & Limitations
Of 25 emails sent, 21 were opened (84%), 19 clicked the link (76% of those sent), and 15 submitted simulated sensitive data (60% of those sent). The team cross-checked the link with VirusTotal and found only 1 of several security vendors flagged it as phishing, underscoring that user awareness matters alongside technical detection. The study proposes a layered defense — awareness training, SPF/DKIM/DMARC email authentication, and a GRC approach aligned with NDPR and ISO/IEC 27001 — as recommendations, not implemented or certified controls.
Lessons Learned
Several participants who clicked the link stopped short of submitting data once asked for financial details — suggesting awareness can kick in mid-interaction, not just at first contact, which shaped how the awareness guide was written.
